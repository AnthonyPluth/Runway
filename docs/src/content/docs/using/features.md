The [home page](/Runway/) has the short list. This is what each part does and where its settings live.

Runway's pages, in the sidebar (or the tab bar on a phone): **Overview**, **Transactions** (All and To review), **Budget**, **Recurring**, **Reports**, then **Net worth** (Summary, Investments, Equity and Retirement) and **Churning**, and **Settings** at the bottom.

**On a phone** (a narrow screen, or a phone turned sideways), every page and editor is there, laid out for the screen: a tab bar at the bottom (Recurring, Net worth, Churning and Settings are under **More**), sheets that rise from the bottom, rows that stack, wide tables that keep their main columns (the rest fold under each row or are left out), and buttons big enough to tap. On a touch screen, actions that a mouse shows on hover (Split, a row's checkbox) are always there.

Settings has these tabs: **Accounts**, **Connections** (SimpleFIN, Plaid, the browser extension for Amazon, Target, Costco and Carta, and the optional services: OpenRouter, Realie, Finnhub and Logo.dev), **Categories**, **Rules**, **Notifications** and **Data** (backup and restore, AI assistants and the version). Until a bank is connected, Settings opens on Connections.

## Cash-flow forecast

On the Overview page.

- **Day-by-day projection** of your forecast account for 30 days to 6 months, with the lowest point called out in plain language ("Checking stays above $2,084 for the next 120 days").
- **Forecast settings:** click the account name above the balance to choose the account and set the default length. The account can also be chosen by opening it in Settings → Accounts and choosing “Use for the forecast”; the forecast account is marked **Forecast** in that list.
- **Coming up** lists the items a day at a time, under their date, with the account's projected balance under each day's items (a bank settles a day's payments together). On Transactions, filtered to one account, the rows leave the account's name out. Scheduled items, card payments and your budgets move the balance; nothing is estimated from past spending. A budget paid straight from the account comes out a little each banking day (a weekend's or a bank holiday's share on the next one) rather than as an item, so a day's balance is after that day's budgeted spending, which the chart's readout shows as a line.
- **Projected rows look different from posted ones:** on Transactions, the rows under Upcoming · projected have an italic name, and the heading marks them as projected, so they aren’t mistaken for transactions that have posted. Pending bank transactions are real and keep their own “Pending” label in the list below.
- **Credit cards paid the way you pay them:** each card's payment comes out of checking on its due date. Statement balances, closing dates, due dates and minimum payments come straight from the card issuer through Plaid (Liabilities) where the bank shares them, shown read only. For a card without one, enter each statement yourself (closing date, balance, due date and, if you like, the minimum) under **Statement** on the card in Settings → Accounts; Runway keeps the earlier ones. A statement you entered counts until about a month after it closed (the next one's closing date, plus a few days): after that the forecast keeps only its own payment and Overview asks for the latest one. Plaid's statement wins when there's both. You can still correct a statement figure, or the payment itself. By default each statement is paid in full; in Settings → Accounts, next to the account that pays a card, choose **Pay: minimum** (the issuer's minimum, or the one you entered, or, when there's none, the larger of $25 and 1% of the statement plus its interest, the way issuers usually work it out, so paying it never lets the balance grow; the forecast says when it's using this) or **a fixed amount** (what you pay each statement, or the whole statement when it's less). What's left of the statement is what's paid. What's already been paid since the statement closed comes off it, including a payment that has left the paying account but hasn't reached the card yet, when that account can only be paying this card (it pays no other, and every card has its paying account set; a card's credit for a payment can post from about 5 days before it leaves the bank to 2 weeks after, so the two are matched within that window). So does a refund or other credit that has posted since the close (a pending one waits until it posts), so a statement paid off early, or brought to nothing by credits, has no payment in the forecast. New charges since the close never add to it: they're the next statement's. A minimum or fixed payment counts what's been paid toward it but not credits (as issuers do), and is never more than what's left. The rest carries into the next statement (and a payment you edit to more than is owed takes the extra off it), plus a month's interest at the card's APR: the one you enter there, else the issuer's purchase APR through Plaid (without either, a warning says interest isn't counted). While a balance carries there's no grace period, so the cycle's new charges are charged interest too, as if they posted evenly through it. The Overview's card list shows each card's amount still due on its statement, not the statement itself (that's in the amount's tooltip, with what's been paid and credited since it closed), or Paid ✓ once nothing is; click the amount to correct it, and Runway keeps it as the statement it implies, so it still comes down as more is paid ("set" marks it, and reset goes back to the bank's figure). It shows "pays $40.00 of $600.00" for a card that isn't paid in full. The list runs in the order the cards are due, soonest (or overdue) first and paid ones last, and leaves out a card with a $0 balance and nothing left to pay on its statement.
- **Starting balance:** the bank's balance plus whatever is pending on the account, money in and out, so a pending debit isn't left out ("Balance as of today, including −$375.00 pending"). From SimpleFIN, only the last 14 days' pending transactions count, the ones a sync re-reads; Plaid removes the ones that are no longer pending, so all of its count. Some banks' balance already includes pending: banks take pending money out of the available balance, so when it's at or below the balance but hasn't come down by the pending money out, the balance has it already and it isn't added again. Pending money in, like a paycheck, is always added, and so is everything pending when there's no available balance or it's above the balance (an overdraft line counted in it). A pending paycheck already matched to its recurring item counts as paid, so it isn't added again.
- **Future statements** are estimated from what you've planned, not from past spending: for the statement in progress, what's been charged so far (pending charges counted as for the starting balance: SimpleFIN's from the last 14 days only, so one that has since posted under a new id isn't counted twice); then, in each cycle, the budgets paid with the card (each spent on it day by day; this month, what's left of it over the days left), the card's recurring items, and any annual fee. A recurring charge or fee in a budgeted category is part of that budget when the budget is charged to the same card; otherwise it's on the statement and comes off that budget, so it counts once. Without budgets, a card's later statements are only its recurring charges and fees. Paying more than a statement (the current balance, say) takes the extra off the next one, and so do refunds; a credit bigger than the next statement leaves nothing to pay that month and carries on to the one after, as the issuer does. A statement that carries a balance is charged interest as above, on the budgeted charges too. An estimate's asterisk shows what went into it, part by part: hover over the asterisk, or tap it on a phone, or click or tap the payment's name to open the same list under the row (it works from the keyboard too). An amount you've changed is yours, so it loses the asterisk.

- **Annual fees on churning cards:** each open card on the Churning page with an annual fee has its next fee in the forecast, in its anniversary month (the month it was opened), from its first anniversary on. It's a charge on the card, not money out of checking: it's added to the statement it lands on and comes out with that statement's payment. It's dated on the anniversary, the day of the month the card was opened (Feb 29 becomes Feb 28), and lands on whichever statement that day falls in, so it's paid with that statement. Overview's Coming up and Transactions' Upcoming list it as "<card> annual fee" (Fees & Interest), with the payment it's in. There's none for a card that's closed or product changed, planned to be closed or changed before the fee, or without a fee, nor once a charge named like "Annual fee", or one of the fee's amount in Fees & Interest, has posted on the card (from about six weeks before the anniversary), or when you've made a recurring item with "fee" in its name on the card for around then. A fee not charged by its day is still expected, today, until the anniversary month ends. A card that isn't linked to a credit card account here (or whose payments aren't in the forecast) has its fee listed, saying so, without changing any balance. A budget for Fees & Interest covers it instead.
- **Big payments that aren't scheduled:** when the last 90 days have payments over $1,000 from the forecast account that aren't transfers or recurring items, a warning names them so you can add them as recurring items (rent paid by hand, tuition); mark a true one-off "not recurring" and it stops being counted.
- **Budgets:** the forecast spends each budget on its account or card; a note under the chart says how much a month that is. This month's includes what a budget that rolls over carried into it, less what's been spent, over the days left. A subcategory with its own budget and card has that budget spent on its card, and the rest of its parent's budget on the parent's (never below nothing), so the month's total is still the parent's budget. A budget includes its category's recurring items, so they aren't counted twice, and one they cover entirely adds nothing. A budget on an account that isn't in the forecast, or on a card that isn't paid from the forecast account, is left out and the note's tooltip says so. A budget on a card with no statement yet still counts (as do its recurring charges and annual fee, budget or not): its statements are taken to close at each month's end and be paid 25 days later, the way the card is set to be paid (in full unless you've chosen the minimum or a fixed amount), until a statement says otherwise.
- **Business days:** a payment due on a weekend or bank holiday lands on the next business day, and a paycheck on the business day before (Federal Reserve holiday calendar).
- **One-off edits:** click any upcoming amount (dotted underline) to change it for that date only, with Undo; for a repeating item, **From now on** in the same message makes it the item's amount instead (a fixed amount, even if it was learned from the payments). A one-time item's amount changes the item itself. A recurring item’s name opens it in Recurring.
- **Warnings link to their fix:** a card with no statement (or an out-of-date one you entered) opens that card in Settings → Accounts at its Statement; one with no paying account opens Settings → Accounts, and big payments the forecast leaves out open Recurring.
- **Known limitations:** with more than one account in the forecast, a recurring transfer from one to another (to savings, say) only counts as money out unless you add the matching money in on the other account as its own recurring item. Plaid reports the date a statement was issued, which Runway takes as its closing date; for an issuer that posts the statement a day or two after it closes, charges in those days are counted toward the next statement rather than this one.
- **This month:** spending so far against the same point last month, the budgets closest to their limit (a dash for one with nothing spent yet), and the latest transactions (leaving out what you marked Ignore). Money in shows with a +, money out with a −, here and on Transactions.

## Connections

Under Settings → Connections, with each account's source chosen under Settings → Accounts. Until a bank is connected, its two ways in sit side by side, collapsed and opening one at a time: SimpleFIN (recommended: the easiest, about $15 a year) and Plaid (with your own developer keys, saved together with **Save**). Connecting one opens Settings → Accounts, where its accounts are. A service that holds an error (a failed sync, a key the AI, Finnhub or Logo.dev refused) says **Needs attention** and opens, with the problem in a sentence and the service's own words under **Details**. Key fields hide what you type (with a button to show it), aren't offered to the browser's saved passwords, and empty once the key is saved.

- **SimpleFIN or Plaid for each account.** Link a bank or card through Plaid, match its accounts to the ones you already have, and pick where each one's balance and transactions come from. Runway matches the clear ones itself (the last digits, initials or words of the name, a similar balance, and, for the big banks Runway recognizes by name, the same bank: never a Chase card for a Citi one. The bank is the one SimpleFIN and Plaid name, or the account's own name when it names one; a card's own name, like "Sapphire" or "Venture X", doesn't say which bank issued it) and leaves the rest for you. Switching keeps your history, categories and recurring matches: transactions both providers have are matched up so nothing is counted twice.
- Accounts only Plaid can reach can be added on their own, and a card linked through Plaid gets its statements from the issuer whichever provider its transactions come from. A mortgage or student loan linked through Plaid gets its interest rate, monthly payment and payoff date from the lender the same way (Plaid Liabilities), for the retirement planner; other loans take them from you in Settings → Accounts.
- **Delete any account** from its row in Settings → Accounts (“Delete account…”), synced ones too. It takes the account's transactions, statements, investment data, recurring items, the rules that only apply to it and how it's paid (a card's payment plan and APR); budgets, homes' loans, churning cards and cards paid from it let go of it. It stays deleted: syncs leave it out (a Plaid account it was linked to is set not to be used). “N deleted accounts · Restore” at the bottom of the list brings one back with the next sync, with whatever history the bank still offers. Restoring brings back the account, not what was deleted with it. An account with more than 50 transactions asks for its name to be typed before it's deleted. Hidden accounts sit in a collapsed “N hidden accounts · Show” line above it. Download a backup first if you might want what's deleted back.
- **Rename or hide an account** with the buttons at the top of its row in Settings → Accounts. A hidden account is left out of lists, totals and the forecast, and its transactions are kept; hiding offers Undo, and “Show again” on it (under the hidden accounts) brings it back.
- **Automatic sync** once a day at 7 AM in Runway's time zone (America/New_York unless you set `TZ`), SimpleFIN and Plaid together, late enough for overnight ACH. At 6:30 AM Runway asks Plaid to fetch from the banks (Plaid's Transactions Refresh add-on), so the 7 AM sync is current. Opening Runway only catches up a day's sync that was missed. The **Sync now** button (the circular arrow beside the sync status in the sidebar, or in the More menu on a phone) syncs whenever you press it: SimpleFIN every time, and Plaid, whose quota is small, only if it hasn't been asked that day. A connection's own Sync button in Settings always asks Plaid, for everything that connection has (bank transactions and card statements, and investments too if it has them); a connection that has both keeps a problem with either until that side syncs again. The button shows only once a bank is connected.

## Recurring

On the Recurring page, right under Budget (the old Budget → Bills & income link still opens it). Items are grouped into Money in and Money out, late ones first and then by when each is next due (“in 9 days”, “tomorrow”, “3 days late”), with what each group comes to a month under it (“≈ $2,400 a month out”: weekly is 52 a year, every two weeks 26, and so on; paused and one-time items aren’t counted). Each row wears its account's bank as a small badge on its logo, as on Transactions. It works without a bank too, for items you add by hand.

- Paychecks, mortgage, bills and subscriptions: weekly, every two weeks, twice a month, monthly, quarterly, twice a year, yearly, or on specific dates (property tax on April 15 and October 15, say).
- **One-time items:** choose **Once** under How often for money you expect on one date only (a tax refund, a deposit coming back, a big bill you know about). The forecast has it on that date, late if it hasn't come yet, and it shows as missed if it never does. It only matches a transaction within 5 days of its date, so its merchant text doesn't claim later payments to the same place.
- Transactions are **matched automatically** to their recurring item by merchant text, on its account and the same way the money moves, whatever the amount. An item can have several texts, one per line (“acme” and “online transfer from savings” for pay that sometimes comes through another bank), and an amount range (“Only amounts between $1,500 and $7,000”) to leave out other payments with the same text. Items from before ranges existed got the range they used to match (30% either side of a fixed amount, 60% of one learned from the payments); widen or clear it to match more. Its matched transactions say which you linked and which matched automatically.
- **Logos:** each bill or paycheck shows its merchant's logo (the last matched transaction's), on Recurring and in Upcoming. Click it on Recurring to choose another, by the item's name, the same way as on Transactions: one of Logo.dev's matches, a website's, none, or Runway's own pick again.
- **Category:** an item can have a category (More options → Category), and so its icon. A transaction you link to the item by hand takes that category, replacing the one it had (a split transaction keeps its parts' categories). One matched automatically takes it only when it has none of its own, and says "its recurring item" set it. An item without a category changes nothing, and unlinking never changes a category. Renaming or removing a category in Settings moves its items with it (or, when it's removed without a replacement, leaves them without one).
- **Linking by hand:** link a transaction on Transactions with its repeat icon (on a phone, “Link to a recurring item” in the transaction's details: the row shows the icon only once it's linked), whatever its category (a transfer that’s part of your pay, say). When none of the item’s texts is on it, Runway asks whether to also match its text from now on. A link you make on a pending transaction, or marking it as not recurring, stays when it posts, from SimpleFIN or Plaid, as its category, name, note and split do.
- **Pause, end and skip:** in an open item, **Pause** takes it out of the forecast (and out of matching and missed payments) until you **Resume** it; **Ends on** (under More options) is the last day it can come; **Skip the next one** leaves out just its next date, which Overview's Coming up lists as “skipped” with its reset (Undo or “Put it back” takes it back too). The date an item **repeats from** can be any date it falls on; when it's next due is beside it.
- **Missed payments** are listed at the top of Recurring (and on Overview) under Needs attention, with how late each is. **Link a transaction** lists the unlinked payments on its account around that date at about its amount, to link the right one right there; **Skip this one** says it isn't coming (Undo brings it back).
- **Paid in parts:** the payments for one date are added up. When money in (a paycheck in two deposits), or money out paid less than half (a small charge near a renewal), comes to less than a fixed amount by more than 10%, the rest is still expected, on the date or today if that’s passed, until the payment’s window closes (4 days either side for twice a month, 6 for monthly); after that it isn’t, and nothing is counted late. A bill that just came in cheaper, or one whose amount is learned from the payments, is simply done. Runway **flags payments that didn't happen** only when nothing came.
- When a fixed amount’s last few payments (since you last set it) all came to something else, more than 5% or $2 off (whichever is more), the same way and close to each other, its row says “Usually $87.40” and the item offers that amount instead (“Use $87.40”) or the average of the last three from now on; the forecast keeps the amount you set until you take one.
- Adding one asks for a name, money out or money in (a bill or a paycheck), a positive amount, how often and the date it repeats from (today unless you change it); the account, the amount to forecast (the fixed amount, the last payment or the average of the last three), the merchant texts (text in the bank's description), the end date and the amount range are under More options, whose line says what's set there while it's closed. Each field's help is in its tooltip.
- Suggestions for recurring items it spots in your history, on your bank accounts and credit cards alike (“Spotted in your history”, with the range when the amounts vary; a card’s is added on that card and shows on its estimated statements): first on the page while you have no items, then a quiet “N spotted · Show” line under them. **Add** adds one as it is (Undo removes it), **Edit first** fills the form so you can adjust it; **Not recurring** hides a suggestion, and “N dismissed · Show” lists those so you can Restore one to be offered again.

## Transactions and categorization

- **Shorter merchant names:** a bank's text is tidied to the merchant: card processors' prefixes ("SQ *", "TST*") and store numbers come off, and so do the ACH and bill-pay words a bank adds after the merchant, so "Target Cach Tran Cash" and "Direct Deposit Target Debitach Tran" show as Target and "Lakeside Bank Baweb Pay Cash" as Lakeside Bank. Only words that are clearly the bank's are cut ("Apple Cash" stays Apple Cash), and the bank's full text is still under the transaction's details. Transactions synced before got the shorter names on upgrading, except ones you renamed; recurring items, rules and logo choices made from a long name still find the merchant by its shorter one.
- **Brands' own names:** a big merchant gets its brand's name instead of the bank's text, so "AMZN Mktp US\*2K3AB1", "Amazon.com\*XYZ" and "AMZN Digital" show as Amazon, "WM Supercenter #123" as Walmart, "SQ \*Starbucks" as Starbucks and "Target T-1234" as Target. A brand's own products stay apart (Prime Video, Audible, Amazon Prime, Uber Eats and Uber One aren't folded into Amazon or Uber). It's careful on purpose, since a wrong brand is worse than a long name: only when the bank's text starts with the brand, never for a payment to it ("Amazon Corp Syf Paymnt"), a part of the business worth telling apart ("Costco Gas", "Apple Cash"), a word other businesses use too, a delivery app that names the restaurant, or a merchant Plaid already named; and a name you or a rule gave a transaction stays. The bank's text is still under the transaction's details, with **Use the bank's name** to put it back: just for that transaction, or for all of that brand's transactions and the syncs from now on (and **Use "Amazon"** to go back), with Undo. Transactions synced before got the brands' names on upgrading, except ones you renamed; rules, recurring items, logo choices and dismissed suggestions made from the bank's name still find them.
- **Rules** (Settings → Rules) with conditions and actions: when the merchant contains, is or starts with some text, the amount is in a range, the money goes out or comes in, or it's in a particular account, then set a category, rename the merchant, split it by percentages ("Costco: 70% Groceries, 30% Household") or put it in Review. When several rules match, the most specific one wins (more conditions first, then “is” before “starts with” before “contains”, then longer text), separately for each thing a rule does. A preview shows what a rule would match before you save it; a rule needs at least one condition, and a split has to add up to 100%. **Apply to past transactions** is off unless you tick it, and then saving first says how many transactions it would change and asks, with Undo afterwards (as **Apply** on a saved rule does). Removing a rule has an Undo too. When you pick a category, the change's toast offers **Always for** the merchant (with what the rule would match and how many more it would change: the merchant’s other transactions in another category, except ones you categorized yourself or split), beside Undo, including after you apply an AI suggestion or set one merchant's transactions together, so nothing writes a rule behind your back. Built-in heuristics handle card payments and sweeps.
- **Optional AI categorization** through OpenRouter (key under Settings → Connections; it uses OpenRouter’s free-models router unless you pick another model there, and only providers that don’t keep or train on what they’re sent, for this and for an order’s items; with none available nothing is sent and the error says so, and a free model that answers in a format Runway can’t read gets an error suggesting a paid one), guided by examples of how you've categorized before and shown as High, Medium or Low (the percent on hover). For each new merchant it's sent the payee and the bank's description with one recent amount, date and account type (never account names or numbers), plus your category names and some payees you've categorized, as examples. An empty model field means the default, shown in it. Confident answers can be applied during sync; the rest wait for you, and it can propose new categories when nothing fits. On To review, while anything waits there, **Suggest categories** lists the five biggest merchants (**Show N more** for the rest), with **Apply all High**; a merchant you **Skip** isn't asked about again unless you ask again including the skipped ones. Every AI call is logged. Every button that asks the AI (**Suggest categories**, **Suggest categories** on an order, **Fill in the rest** on a card, and **Suggest budgets**) is an outline button with a sparkles icon; on a phone it shows just the icon, and its name is still read out by a screen reader.
- **Split transactions:** a $100 run to Target can be $60 Groceries and $40 Shopping. The split editor shows what's left to assign, puts it on a part in one click, or splits the amount evenly; Save waits until the parts add up to the cent. Budgets, reports and category filters count each part on its own; pick a single category again and the transaction goes back together. Filtered by a category (from a budget, say), a split transaction shows only its part in that category (“−$42.44 of $60.88”), so the list adds up to what the budget counts. A category picked for it there, one at a time or for several ticked at once, changes only that part (an order's items in it, when an order split it); once every part has the same category, the transaction takes it whole. Its receipt shows just that category's items, with their estimated share of the tax and shipping, and the rest a click away.
- **Amazon, Target and Costco orders:** a small browser extension (installed from Settings → Connections) reads your orders with the sign-in already in your browser, and Runway splits each card charge by what you bought: tax and shipping shared out, each Amazon shipment matched to its own items. Items are categorized by the AI if you've set it up, and a category you pick for an item sticks for next time. Picking one category for the transaction itself (one at a time or in bulk) sets every item of its order to it too, so the charge stays whole, and Undo puts the items back as they were. See [Browser extension](/Runway/using/browser-extension/).
- **Bulk editing:** tick transactions (shift-click for a range) to give them a category, rename their merchant or accept their categories at once. With every transaction shown ticked, **Select all 212** takes in every one the filters match, not just those loaded so far. On a phone, **Select** makes a tap anywhere on a row tick it, and the bar is just the count, **Categorize** and **More** for the rest.
- **To review:** a transaction waiting with a category (the AI's, a rule's or what it was last time) has a ✓ beside its amount (**Accept** on a phone) that keeps it as it is; **Accept all ≥ 90%** does that for every one Runway is that sure of. A transaction leaves the list as soon as you decide, and comes back with an error if it couldn't be saved. Changes made one after another share one toast (“3 changed · Undo”). On a computer, **j**/**k** (or the arrows) move between rows, **Enter** accepts (or opens the category when there's none), **c** opens the category, **i** marks it Ignore, **t** Transfer, **s** opens the split editor and **Esc** lets go. Without an AI key, **Group by merchant** shows one row per merchant with one category for all of them.
- **Picking a category:** type to narrow the list (“gro” for Groceries). Arrows move, Enter picks, Esc closes. On a phone it opens as a sheet.
- **Filters:** the search matches the merchant, the bank's text, the category, your note, or an amount typed as a number ("59.28"). Beside it are the account, the category and the dates (This month, Last month, Last 3 months, Next month, Next 3 months, This year, All time, or your own from and to, which can be ahead of today), and under **More**, the kind (money in, money out or transfers) and an amount range, in or out. Each filter under More shows as a chip you can remove, and **Clear filters** clears them all. The line under the filters says how many transactions match and what they add up to, leaving out transfers as the day totals do. The filters are part of the page's address (`#transactions?q=rent&from=2026-03-01`), so a reload, Back or a bookmark keeps them; To review keeps its own.
- **Details and editing:** click a transaction's name, or the › at the end of its row, to open its details: a panel on the right, or a sheet from the bottom on a phone. Change its name, category, date, amount or note there; each saves when you leave the field, with Undo. **Exclude from reports and budgets** marks it Ignore, and **This is a transfer** gives it the Transfer category. The details also show the account, where it came from, the bank's own text and who set the category, with its split, recurring item and receipt. A date or amount you change on a synced transaction stays yours: later syncs update the bank's figure, which shows on hovering over "edited", instead. A pending transaction's date and amount stay the bank's until it posts.
- **Adding by hand:** **Add**, beside the heading, records a transaction the bank doesn't have, such as cash: its account, date, name, amount (money in or out), category and note. It counts like any other in reports and budgets; the account's balance (where the forecast starts) still comes from the bank. If the bank later brings in the same transaction, Runway doesn't match the two, so delete yours from its details. Deleting asks first and can't be undone.
- **Categories** (Settings → Categories) have one level of subcategories, each with an emoji and a color. Click a category's emoji to change it: search by name (“coffee”, “car”), type or paste any emoji, or pick one from the grid. On a phone, tapping it opens just a field and your keyboard (use its emoji key), and picking an emoji from the keyboard saves it and closes the field. Removing a category that nothing uses happens at once, with Undo; one with transactions, rules, a budget or order items asks first, saying what happens to each (its transactions go to Review or to a category you pick). Anything uncategorized waits on the To review tab; there is also search and filtering. The list is grouped by day with each day's total (transfers and card payments left out), and loads more as you scroll. What you've categorized **Ignore** (or one of its subcategories) stays off the All tab unless you choose **Show** on the “N ignored” line, or pick Ignore in the category filter.
- **Merchant logos** for merchants Plaid knows, downloaded once from Plaid and served by Runway itself. With a free Logo.dev publishable key (Settings → Connections), merchants Plaid has no logo for get one from Logo.dev by their website (Plaid's, or for about 95 big names like Target, Amazon and Walmart, one Runway knows): downloaded during a sync, re-checked monthly, served by Runway. The same key gives each bank or card its logo: by website for big banks Runway recognizes by name (Chase, Citi, Capital One, American Express and so on), else by the institution's name as SimpleFIN or Plaid gives it; without it, accounts show a letter. Runway keeps no list of which bank issues which card, so a card's own name ("Venture X", "Double Cash") doesn't pick a logo: an account with no institution shows its letter unless its name names the bank. A credit card payment has no merchant logo, even when it names a store ("Payment to Costco Anywhere Visa" isn't Costco): it shows the logo of the bank of the card it pays, when Runway can tell which card (the card's own payment, a credit of the same amount on one card within a couple of weeks, or the only card paid from that account in Settings → Accounts), else the bank's logo of the account it left. To use a different logo for an account, or none, open the account in Settings → Accounts and click its logo.

## Budget and reports

- Monthly budgets per category with pace markers (an even pace counts today as half gone); what's still coming this month (recurring items not yet paid, on cards too) is shaded on the bar after what's spent. Click a category's name or what it spent to jump to its transactions, with what's still coming in Upcoming above them. A subcategory's budget shows its figures under its parent's bar, in red when it's over, and a parent's budget grows to cover its subcategories' when one of theirs is set higher. An income category can have a budget too, for what you expect to come in: it has its own Income group, green, with what's still to come and how much of it is scheduled, and it never counts toward what's budgeted or the forecast.
- **The card a category goes on** is set per category in Settings → Categories, with or without a budget, subcategories too (Automatic uses the parent's, else the account used most). The Budget page shows its bank's logo on each category's emoji (on a computer or tablet; not on a phone).
- **Rollover**, budget by budget: what's left at the end of a month is added to the next (going over isn't carried). It's switched on and off from the ⋯ menu on a budget's row on the Budget page (it shows on hover, always on a touch screen); a budget that rolls over says **Rolls over** beside its name, and that label doesn't toggle it.
- **Suggest budgets** works out a monthly amount for each category that has a budget, and for each top-level category with no budget anywhere in it: the median of what it spent (subcategories included) in each of the last 6 full months, or the recurring bills the forecast has for it next month (or in the month you're looking at, if it's still to come; past the forecast's year ahead, the last month it reaches) when they're more, rounded up to the next $5. A month it spent nothing in counts as $0, so a one-off doesn't set the budget, and months before your first transaction don't count. Each suggestion shows what it's from and the budget it has now; apply them one by one or all at once. Nothing is saved until you do, and a budget is never suggested away.
- **Reports** has five views: **Cash flow** (a Sankey chart of where each month's money went), **Over time** (spending by category, merchant or account over 6, 12 or 24 months, with each one's change from last month and a year ago), **Merchants** (ranked by what you spent, each with its months and transactions; the search covers every merchant, not just the top 100), **Income** (money in, money out, what's left over and your savings rate) and **Breakdown** (a treemap you click into, from categories to subcategories to merchants to the transactions behind them). Over time and Income start at the first full month of your history (the month of your first transaction if it's on the 1st, otherwise the month after, unless that would leave only this month), so months before there was any history, or only part of one, don't count as $0 or pull the averages down, and Over time's averages are of the full months, not the one under way. The month under way is drawn faint and marked "so far"; Over time compares it with last month (and a year ago) up to the same day ("vs Sep 1–3"), and a category with nothing to compare with says New. Only a month that's over is flagged red for spending more than came in. Income's tiles are this year so far, whatever months its chart shows. Categories wear the colors they have on Budget, in every view. Click a column, a band or bar in the Sankey, a block's transaction or a merchant's row to open those transactions on Transactions, already filtered; on a touch screen the first tap shows the figure and a second opens it. Cash flow, Over time and Breakdown have their numbers as a table too, and on a phone the Cash flow and Breakdown tables start open. If a report can't load it says so with **Retry**, and while it reloads the last figures stay on screen dimmed.

## Investments

On Net worth → Investments.

- Holdings and performance for brokerage and retirement accounts, modeled on Ghostfolio: time-weighted return, a comparison with the S&P 500, gain per holding and allocation.
- **Near-real-time prices** while the market is open, with each holding's gain today. With a free [Finnhub](https://finnhub.io) key (Settings → Connections) they become real trades, pushed the moment they happen for up to 50 of your stocks and ETFs. Runway holds one connection to Finnhub for the whole app and your browser never sees the key. Yahoo takes over for anything else, or if Finnhub can't be reached.
- Positions from SimpleFIN, or optionally from Plaid for accounts SimpleFIN only knows the balance of. Accounts that neither can see into, like some 401(k)s, can be **tracked by hand**: enter shares and your contribution split, and Runway invests each new deposit accordingly. They're in a collapsed “Accounts without automatic holdings” section at the bottom of Investments: a line for each (“balance only · Enter holdings”, “entered by hand · Edit holdings”) opens the entry form, and warns when the funds you entered are off the synced balance; while it's closed, its summary says how many need updating.
- **Total gain** is each holding's value less its cost basis, and **Return** the time-weighted return over the period you pick; gains are green and losses coral with a real minus (−$40.00). Each holding's change since the last close (dollars and percent) has its own **Today** column on a wide screen; on a phone or tablet it's a “Today +$12.50 (+0.40%)” line under the holding's shares, colored the same way. Live prices re-price the totals and holdings; the charts are the last sync's (a "Charts as of last sync" tag says so), and "Holdings updated …" turns amber once they're more than two days old.
- Editable cost basis, per share: the figure has a dotted underline (and a pencil on touch screens); leave the box empty to go back to the institution's figure.
- A reload that fails (another period, say) keeps the numbers on screen under "Couldn't refresh · Retry", and the period picker stays on the period they're for.
- **Logos** beside each holding, with a Logo.dev key (Settings → Connections): a stock's by its ticker, and a fund's by its fund family (Vanguard, iShares, Fidelity and so on, read from the fund's name) when Logo.dev has none for the ticker. Runway downloads them when investments sync, and a holding without one shows its first letter. To use a different logo for a holding (one of Logo.dev’s matches, a website’s, or none, which shows its letter), click its logo, the same way as on Transactions; **Automatic** goes back to Runway’s own pick. The choice is kept for that stock or fund across all your accounts.
- **Which accounts count:** every investment account does, unless you hide it in Settings → Accounts, which leaves it out of the holdings, totals, charts and the retirement plan alike. There's no separate switch on the Investments page. An account that both SimpleFIN and Plaid send (the one you matched, or the same last digits at the same institution) is counted once, as its Plaid one; a different account at the same institution, like a 401(k) beside a brokerage, counts on its own.

## Retirement

On Net worth → Retirement.

- A retirement planner that projects your investments year by year to the age you plan for, across 1,000 simulated market paths, with the chance your money lasts and a word for it (Likely at 85% or more, Uncertain from 70%, Unlikely below; hover it for the thresholds). It says what it starts from ("Starting from $62,500 invested today (2 accounts)"), and its chart marks today and has the calendar year under each age. A retirement age below your age now, or planning to an age before retirement, is flagged under the field instead of projected. Its assumptions (spending, saving, return, Social Security, pensions, one-off events, selling a home) start from your own numbers and keep whatever you change; **Start over** (after a confirmation) goes back to Runway's figures. Until you enter your own dates the results are muted and marked "Starting estimate · edit below", and with nothing invested yet the tab points you to Investments instead. It plans from your investments, before tax, in today's dollars; a switch shows the figures in future dollars instead (grown by the inflation you set), and remembers your choice in that browser. Future dollars are each year's own price tags: every year's figure is today's grown by inflation for that many years, so later years look bigger and the curve bends upward, but the plan is the same: the chance your money lasts, the age it runs out and every sale and payment are worked out once, in today's dollars, and don't change. What you enter (spending, saving, income such as Social Security, one-off events) is in today's dollars and keeps pace with inflation; a loan's payment and balance are fixed dollar amounts, so in today's dollars they shrink each year.
- **Assumptions you set once** (each person's birth year, how long to plan for, income in retirement such as Social Security and pensions, and the returns, ups and downs and inflation) are under **Assumptions**, folded to a one-line summary once they're filled in and open while something's missing.
- **Home equity and equity comp.** The chart stacks what you'd have outside your investments on top of them: each home's (and other asset's) value less what's still owed on it, and company equity that has vested, each until the year you sell it into the plan (from then its proceeds are in the investments, so it isn't counted twice). They only pay for retirement once sold. A value grows by the asset's yearly change from the Net worth page; with none set, and for equity (at today's share price), it keeps pace with inflation, so it holds its value in today's dollars. A home is assumed to keep up with inflation at least, so a yearly change below it counts as keeping pace; other assets follow theirs down. Vehicles lose value, so the plan leaves them out and doesn't list them; a loan against one is still paid from spending until it's paid off.
- **Student and personal loans.** A loan account that isn't against a home or vehicle (and counts in net worth) is listed in the plan as a loan, with what's owed: a debt, never sold. It's paid down on its terms like a mortgage (the lender's through Plaid, or what you set under Settings → Accounts), and its payment is handled like any other loan's below: taken off spending once it's paid off, added while it's paid if it went out as a transfer, or left as it is when Runway can't tell.
- **Where the starting figures come from.** Spending is your average over the last six full months, counted the way Reports counts it: every category except transfers and income, plus money out you haven't categorized; a month with more money back than out counts as a month of no spending. With less history than that (a bank linked lately), it's the average of the full months Runway has transactions for, so three months of history aren't spread over six; the month history starts in counts only when it starts on the 1st. Saving is what went into your investments in the last 12 months, so a rollover or a one-off lump sum counts too (the page says so, and says when your investment history is shorter than a year); change it if that isn't what you usually save.
- **Two people:** each person's saving stops at their own retirement, and spending comes from the investments only once both have retired. Until then the pay of whoever's still working is assumed to cover your living costs.
- **Selling a home or equity into the plan:** tick a home, other asset or company's equity and choose the year to sell it, this year or later (a year that has passed since you chose it is counted as this year, and the page says so). A home's value grows by its yearly change from the Net worth page, and the loan against it is paid down month by month to what will still be owed that year, using the loan's interest rate and monthly payment: the lender's, through Plaid, for mortgages and student loans, or what you enter on the loan in Settings → Accounts (an auto loan, or a loan from SimpleFIN). Left empty, the payment is the typical month's payments into the loan account lately. Without an interest rate the plan counts today's balance and says so. Equity counts what will have vested by then, at today's share price kept up with inflation, including grants that haven't vested at all yet: each grant goes on vesting on its schedule (from its grant date when it has no vesting start), and where Carta reported what's vested, it goes on from that figure, all vested when the schedule says (the list shows what's vested today and what it comes to once all vested); options past their expiration date count only the shares exercised from them. Proceeds are before selling costs (often 6–8% of a home's price) and tax. Hover over the estimate to see what it assumes.
- **A loan's payment ends.** Your spending includes your loan payments, so once a loan whose payment is known (as above) is paid off (from the following year) or its asset is sold (from that year), its payment comes off your spending in the projection. That's when the payment was counted as spending (money out within about 10% of it, in a spending category or uncategorized, in at least four of the last six full months, or, with less history than that, in every full month Runway has (at least two, or the one month when the payment names the lender), and the ones naming the lender or the loan when there are any; one naming it can be up to half again the payment, as a mortgage paid with its escrow for taxes and insurance is). A payment categorized as a transfer (common when the loan account is synced too) isn't in your spending figure, so the plan adds it to your spending until the loan is paid off or sold instead; that's only when the transfers name the lender or the loan (a transfer to savings of about the same size isn't taken for it). A payment Runway finds neither way (paid in halves, missing from a month) is left as it is. This applies to Runway’s own spending figure. Once you type your own figure it’s taken as it is; “Use Runway’s figure” under it goes back. The page says, for each loan, whether its payment is in your spending and when it ends.

## Net worth and equity

- **Summary:** every account plus homes, vehicles and anything else you own, minus cards and loans, recorded daily. Optional automated home values through Realie (key under Settings → Connections; each home is looked up once a week at most, within the free tier). Once Realie has valued a home with an address it can use and you keep it updated weekly, its value comes from Realie only (Update from Realie, or weekly), with no hand-typed value; vehicles, other assets, homes Realie can't value and homes you don't keep updated are valued by hand.
- **Loans go down between updates.** A loan with an interest rate and a monthly payment (the lender's through Plaid, what you enter under Settings → Accounts, or, with a rate but no payment, the typical month's payments into the loan account lately) is paid down month by month from its last balance, so a home's equity grows even when the bank doesn't send a new balance. Without an interest rate (0% counts as one) or a payment, the balance stays as the bank last reported it. The retirement planner starts from the same paid-down balance.
- **Change over 1M, 3M or 1Y** (“+$4,210 (+2.9%) in the last month”, green for a gain and coral for a loss) is measured from the latest snapshot at least that old, and the chart starts from the same snapshot. Snapshots are saved on the days the numbers are looked at, so after a gap the change can cover longer than its label; when it's more than a few days longer, the page says since when (“+$4,200 since Aug 10”). Until there are two days of history, a line says it builds up each day you open Runway. Pointing at the chart (or the arrow keys, once it has focus) shows that day's net worth in the headline.
- **What makes it up:** each kind of asset keeps its color in the bar and before its heading. Debts that are all $0 (cards paid off) show as “Nothing owed”. A reload that fails after a change keeps the numbers under “Couldn't refresh · Retry”.
- **Equity:** stock options (ISO/NSO), RSUs, restricted stock and shares, each with its vesting schedule (cliff, monthly or quarterly), exercise price and exercises. Runway shows what has vested and what's still to come at each company's latest share price (the last one you entered, before tax), and counts the vested part in net worth. A price about three months old or more is flagged (“price from 7 months ago”). The vesting chart has a line per company, with the smaller ones added up as Other past four. The grant form previews the schedule as you type (“25% on Jan 2027, then monthly until Jan 2030”); on a phone each grant is a card. A Carta sync or read that failed says so, with a link to fix it in Settings. Enter grants by hand, or read them from **Carta** with the browser extension (with your Carta sign-in, like Amazon and Target) or with Carta's Portfolio API if Carta approves your app.

## Churning

Credit cards and bank accounts opened for their sign-up bonuses, for you and your partner, on four tabs under the 5/24, annual fee and bonus tiles: **Overview** (Upcoming, Planned, Best card for… and Rewards), Cards, Benefits and Bank bonuses. Overview is the page's address (`#churning`) and Cards is `#churning/cards`. Deadlines (an annual fee, a bonus's spending, a bank bonus's due date, an Upcoming item) are red within 7 days or once overdue and amber within 30; a bonus's spending bar takes the same color, and hovering it says how much a day the rest needs. A status (received, missed, a plan that's due) is a filled label, and an attribute (Business, AU) a plain outline.

- **Cards found on your accounts:** every credit card account Runway already has that isn't a churning card yet appears under "Found on your accounts" (on larger screens), pre-filled from what the bank reported: whose it is, the bank (matched to the ones Churning knows, else Other), the card's name cleaned of the last digits and network words, the latest annual fee (from transactions named like "Annual fee"), a business card when its name says so, and the account's spending linked. **Add** opens the add-card form to review (nothing is saved until you save it); **Dismiss** hides one, and you can bring it back from the "dismissed" list. Banks don't say when a card was opened, so the date is the account's first transaction in Runway, marked "on or before": change it to the real day, since 5/24 and the bonus rules depend on it.
- **Adding a card:** the form keeps the essentials up front (whose card, the bank, the card's name, the day it was opened and the annual fee) and puts the rest in sections that start closed: Earning rates, Sign-up bonus, Benefits, Plan and More details. A closed section says what's in it ("3 rates", "75k Ultimate Rewards after $4,000 in 3 months"), and one opens by itself when the AI fills something in it or a save is refused because of it. Add checks the required fields (marked with a star) and says which are missing; a refusal from the server shows by the Add button and in its section, and on a phone Add stays at the bottom of the form. Benefits can be added with the card (from the quick-add list or typed in) and are saved with it; a lounge's guests, what a benefit is worth to you and its reminders are set from the card's Edit afterwards. The annual fee always posts in the account's anniversary month (the month the card was opened, or for a product change, the month the original card was), so there's no month to choose; a month set on a card by an older version is ignored.
- **Fill in the rest:** with an OpenRouter key set (Settings → Connections), the add and edit card forms offer a button that asks the model about the card: its family, what it earns, earning rates by your categories, annual fee, travel portal, benefits (credits with their amount and how often they reset, lounge access and guests, status) and, when you're adding a card, the current public sign-up bonus (amount, spend and months); the sections that get something open for you to check. Card offers change often, so by default it searches the web first (OpenRouter's web search, preferring the bank's own page) and lists the pages it used under the suggestions ("From: chase.com, …") so you can check them. The search costs extra on top of the model: about $4 per 1,000 results with OpenRouter's own search engine and at most 10 results a card, so up to about 4¢, plus the model's tokens for reading them (a model's built-in search, where OpenRouter uses one, is priced by its provider). Switch it off under Settings → Connections → AI categorization to answer from the model's memory alone, which is cheaper but often out of date, and is labelled so. If the search fails, the error says so. Only the bank's name and the card's name are sent: no account, owner, balance or transaction. Anything that isn't one of your currencies or categories is dropped, and the rest fills only what's still empty, marked "Suggested by AI, check before saving". Card lookups use their own model (Claude Haiku 4.5 by default, since it calls the web search reliably and is cheap), set under Settings → Connections → AI beside the categorization model. Models can still be wrong; nothing is saved until you add the card (or choose Save these on an existing one).
- **5/24** per person (authorized-user, business and product-changed cards left out) with the day each card stops counting.
- **Annual fees** coming up (keep, downgrade or close?), or your plan for each card: keep it and it stops asking; downgrade or close it and you're reminded before the fee, with a Done that updates the card. The tile at the top shows the fees due within 30 days and how many cards they are, and each card's row shows its fee net of its credits. The fee is also in the cash-flow forecast, through the linked card's statement payment, unless you plan to close or downgrade the card first.
- **Bonus spending and direct deposits** followed from linked accounts, and when a bonus can be earned again by each bank's commonly reported rule (a rule of thumb, overridable).
- **Card benefits and credits**, what's left this period (Mark used asks how much of a credit, the rest by default), and the annual fee net of the ones you use.
- **Bank accounts:** safe-to-close days and fee-waiver reminders, and bank bonus money per year (usually reported as interest). On the Bank bonuses tab, bonuses that are paid or closed fold into a collapsed "N paid or closed" group under the ones still in progress. The Cards tab lists each card's fee, bonus, plan and benefits; what it earns in each category is in the card's edit form, and **Best card for…** answers which to use.
- **Undo and asking first:** what can be put back says so in a toast with Undo (checking off a to-do or plan, dropping a planned item, removing a balance, marking a benefit used or taking a use off, dismissing a found card). What can't (a card with its to-dos, benefits and rates, a bank bonus, a planned item or a benefit, deleted for good) asks first in a dialog saying what goes with it.
- **Your own to-dos** (snoozable), and the cards and bank bonuses you want next, with what's in the way (5/24, bonus rules, a credit score you want first) and the earliest day to apply.
- **The best card for a purchase** by category, including, for travel, rates earned only through the issuer's travel portal, and **estimated points** by program (airline and hotel programs each their own) at estimated values you can change.

## Everyday

- **A setup checklist** for new users (connect a bank, choose your forecast account, add paychecks and bills, set budgets), each step with a short line on what it means. The later steps wait until there's an account; while accounts from Plaid wait for a decision, it points at Settings → Accounts. Dismissing it offers Undo.
- A dark interface that works on a phone as well as a desktop, with a tab bar at the bottom on phones, and it **installs as an app** on your iPhone's Home Screen. See [Deployment](/Runway/start/deployment/#on-your-phone).
- **Push notifications** (Settings → Notifications) on your phone or computer: a card payment coming up, the forecast getting low, a recurring payment that didn't show up, a large charge, syncing that keeps failing, the browser extension's daily import waiting for you to sign in to a store, or, for Churning, an annual fee or sign-up bonus deadline coming up, a card you planned to downgrade or close, a credit about to reset, or a card you planned that you can apply for now. Each alert is sent once. With sign-in, notifications are each person's own: their devices and what they're told about, which nobody else sees. Turn them off on a device from that device, or remove it from the Devices list.
- **Backups** as a single file, restorable into either database (Settings → Data, or the command line). Settings shows what a backup holds before you restore it, and keeps a copy of what it replaces in the data directory. A backup from an older version is brought up to date as it's restored; one from before backups recorded their version says its older data may not have been. See [Deployment](/Runway/start/deployment/#backups-migration-and-postgres).
- **AI assistants** can read your data through Runway's [MCP endpoint](/Runway/using/mcp/) once you approve them (Settings → Data lists and disconnects them), and, if you allow it, make changes up to entering a statement's transactions, asking you before each one.
